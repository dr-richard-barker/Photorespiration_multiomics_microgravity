\title[Gas transport predicts the spaceflight transcriptome]{A gas-transport model predicts the \textit{Arabidopsis} spaceflight

\abstract{Plants flown on the International Space Station are grown inside enclosures, and
the atmosphere inside those enclosures is rarely measured. We coupled a validated
lattice-Boltzmann model of leaf boundary-layer gas transport to a Farquhar--von
Caemmerer--Berry model of C$_3$ photosynthesis and asked what an illuminated, sealed canister
does to the CO$_2$ reaching Rubisco. A mass balance with an assumed headspace predicts that
photosynthesis draws a sealed Biological Research in Canisters (BRIC) enclosure toward its
CO$_2$ compensation point within minutes, reducing 12\,h carbon gain to 1\,\% of an
unenclosed control; at a drawn-down 100\,ppm, an assumed value, the Rubisco oxygenation
fraction rises from 28\,\% to 54\,\% at saturating light. Because that drawdown is a mass balance,
it occurs in the ground control as well as in flight. It therefore does not bias the
flight-versus-ground contrast every such experiment reports --- it changes what that contrast
is \emph{about}. We estimate the enclosure effect on metabolite pools to be 22 times the
microgravity effect, which means the spaceflight response is being measured in plants their
own hardware has already carbon-starved.
Contrary to our own starting hypothesis, the microgravity boundary-layer penalty does not
amplify as CO$_2$ falls; it converges, leaving a persistent assimilation deficit at
saturating light of 5--9\,\% at leaf and rosette scale and 26--29\,\% in a dense canopy, and
under 3\,\% at leaf and rosette scale at the experiment's own light level.
We tested these predictions against OSD-522 (BRIC-LED), where the model called eight of
nine transcriptome gene-set responses and four of seven in the proteome, and against four
further OSDR studies, six flight-versus-ground contrasts in all. On the
starvation-over-photosynthesis contrast illumination separated the six completely --- every
illuminated contrast positive, every dark one negative, although the model predicted no
signal in the dark rather than an inverse --- while the predicted gradient across enclosure
types did not hold. NASA's Open Science Data Repository contains no plant metabolome at all
(none of its twelve metabolite-profiling studies is of a plant), so the third omic
layer for pathway integration had to be predicted rather than measured. We argue that
enclosure atmosphere is a first-order, currently unmeasured covariate in plant spaceflight
biology --- not a bias to correct for, but a baseline state to measure --- and set out the
three-arm design and the in-canister measurement that would establish it.}

\keywords{spaceflight, microgravity, photorespiration, \textit{Arabidopsis},
growth hardware, multi-omics}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Interpreting a plant spaceflight experiment means attributing a molecular difference to
microgravity. That attribution rests on an assumption that is almost never tested: that the
flight and ground arms differ in gravity and in nothing else that matters to metabolism.

Plants flown on the International Space Station are grown inside enclosures --- sealed
Petri Dish Fixation Units in BRIC canisters, Petri plates on the cabin wall in CARA, actively
ventilated chambers in VEGGIE and the Advanced Plant Habitat. These enclosures differ by
orders of magnitude in how freely they exchange gas with the cabin, and to our knowledge no
BRIC-class flight has logged the CO$_2$ concentration its plants actually experienced.

The quantity that matters is not the cabin atmosphere but the CO$_2$ mole fraction reaching
Rubisco, $C_c$, which sits at the end of a chain of conductances: from bulk air across the
leaf boundary layer, through the stomata, and through the mesophyll. Only the first of those
is gravity-dependent, through buoyancy-driven convection. Rubisco partitions between
carboxylation and oxygenation as $V_o/V_c = 2\Gamma^*/C_c$ \citep{farquhar1980}, so anything
that lowers $C_c$ raises photorespiration.

Here we ask a question about the enclosure rather than about gravity, and we ask it of the
model before we ask it of any data.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{An illuminated sealed canister is a CO\texorpdfstring{$_2$}{2}-starvation chamber}

Boundary-layer conductance was taken from LunarLeaf-CFD \citep{lunarleaf}, a
lattice-Boltzmann solver validated against four analytical and empirical gates. The enclosure
figures come from two simpler models in the same repository, not from that solver. A
headspace mass balance, with an assumed 30\,mL of air over 3\,cm$^2$ of leaf, draws a lit BRIC
canister from 400\,ppm to near zero in approximately seven minutes; a zero-dimensional
photosynthesis-feedback model puts 12\,h carbon gain at 1\,\% of an unenclosed 1\,$g$ control,
against 90\,\% for a dish sealed with gas-permeable tape of assumed permeance and 100\,\% when
ventilated (Fig.~\ref{fig:hardware}).

The step that matters for interpretation is that this drawdown is a mass balance between
photosynthetic uptake and enclosure volume. It is therefore essentially gravity-independent,
and it happens in the ground control as much as in flight.

\subsection{What that does to Rubisco, and what gravity does not do}

Feeding the boundary-layer conductance into a Farquhar--von Caemmerer--Berry model
\citep{farquhar1980} with in-vivo Rubisco kinetics \citep{bernacchi2001,bernacchi2003}, solved
at saturating irradiance (1{,}000\,\textmu mol m$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$), moves
the leaf along its CO$_2$ response curve: oxygenation fraction rises from
28.5\,\% in ventilated hardware at ambient CO$_2$ to 53.7\,\% in a drawn-down canister
(Fig.~\ref{fig:fvcb}a, Table~\ref{tab:operating}). The canister value is an assumption: the
sealed arms are placed at 100\,ppm, whereas at this light the compensation point is 54\,ppm,
where the fraction would be 61\,\%.

We began from the hypothesis that CO$_2$ starvation would \emph{amplify} the microgravity
boundary-layer penalty. The model says the opposite (Fig.~\ref{fig:fvcb}b). As assimilation
falls toward the compensation point, the flux through the boundary layer falls with it, so
the CO$_2$ drop across that layer, $A/g_{bl}$, tends to zero and the two gravities converge.
What survives is a persistent proportional assimilation deficit of 5--7\,\% at leaf scale,
7--9\,\% at rosette scale and 26--29\,\% in a dense canopy, nearly independent of where in the
drawdown the plant sits (Fig.~\ref{fig:fvcb}c). That insensitivity is the prediction's
strength: it does not require knowing the canister CO$_2$, which nobody measured. Both the
size of the deficit and its insensitivity hold at saturating light only; at the light level
of the experiments tested below the deficit is smaller and does depend on CO$_2$ (see
Limitations).

Propagating both contrasts to metabolite pools makes the asymmetry explicit. The
sealed-versus-ventilated hardware effect is 22 times the flight-versus-ground effect
(Fig.~\ref{fig:fvcb}d) --- and because it acts on both arms, it cancels.

\subsection{A metabolite layer that does not exist}

Pathway-level integration needs a metabolome. We queried all 567 OSDR studies and
cross-tabulated organism against assay type: twelve are metabolite profiling, and every one
is mouse, human, rat or microbial. \textbf{No plant study has a metabolome.}

We therefore predicted one (Fig.~\ref{fig:metabolome}), propagating the modelled fluxes to 21
compounds under one stated assumption --- that at quasi-steady state a pool tracks the flux
feeding it. Every value is labelled as model output in every file. Compounds the model cannot
speak to, including all tricarboxylic-acid intermediates and all fermentation products, are
excluded with the reason recorded rather than filled in.

\subsection{The model called eight of nine transcriptome gene sets}

OSD-522 \citep{osd522} grew \textit{Arabidopsis} in BRIC-LED hardware under 60\,\textmu mol
m$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$ of red and blue light, with six flight and six ground-control shoot
samples. We computed differential expression with PyDESeq2 \citep{pydeseq2}, obtaining 1{,}795
genes at FDR $<0.05$ from 19{,}325 with an adjusted $p$ (20{,}983 measured), and used the deposited proteomics ratios for 5{,}160
proteins (Fig.~\ref{fig:omics}).

Scoring these against KEGG-derived gene sets (Fig.~\ref{fig:blind}), the model's predictions
held for eight of nine sets: the photosystem and light-harvesting apparatus fell
($-0.238$, $p = 3.8\times10^{-6}$; and in the proteome $-0.820$, $p = 5.0\times10^{-4}$),
Rubisco fell ($-0.403$, $p = 4.5\times10^{-3}$), carbon fixation and starch/sucrose
metabolism fell, carbon-starvation DIN markers rose ($+0.685$, $p = 2.6\times10^{-3}$), and
photorespiratory enzymes were not induced. The one mismatch is the whole KEGG glyoxylate and
dicarboxylate map, which contains a good deal besides the C$_2$ cycle. Three of the eight
matches are sets predicted not to change, which pass by not reaching $p<0.05$, on three to
thirteen genes each. In the proteome the predictions held for four of seven testable sets:
carbon fixation, starch/sucrose and Rubisco did not fall significantly.

The sharper test is between sets, which is immune to the global depression of metabolic
transcripts: photorespiratory enzymes sat above Rubisco in the transcriptome
($+0.239$, $p = 6.8\times10^{-3}$) and independently in the proteome
($+0.285$, $p = 4.9\times10^{-2}$). The third set-versus-set test, photorespiration against
carbon fixation as whole KEGG maps, showed no signal in either layer ($-0.020$, $p = 0.95$;
$+0.030$, $p = 0.82$).

\subsection{Pathway integration puts photorespiration in the bottom 8\,\%}

Submitting all three layers to PaintOmics \citep{paintomics4} against KEGG and MapMan
(Fig.~\ref{fig:paintomics}), 97--100\,\% of genes, 97\,\% of proteins and all 21 predicted
compounds mapped. Of 232 pathways tested, 32 reached combined Fisher $p<0.05$ uncorrected,
including photosynthesis ($p = 0.008$), photosynthesis antenna proteins ($p = 0.022$) and
starch and sucrose metabolism ($p = 0.019$). Six survive Benjamini--Hochberg correction at
FDR $<0.05$, led by protein processing in the endoplasmic reticulum, and none of the pathways
the model made a claim about is among them (photosynthesis FDR $= 0.14$). Glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism --- the
photorespiration map --- ranked \emph{214th of 232} at $p = 0.994$, in the bottom
8\,\% and the least enriched of any pathway the model made a claim about. Eighteen
pathways rank below it, nine of them at $p = 1$.

The metabolite hub analysis is the least circular result available here, because the
compounds are predicted but the differentially expressed genes counted around them are
measured. Fifteen compounds entered it: the thirteen submitted as changed, and two glucose
anomers onto which PaintOmics also mapped D-glucose. Seven reached FDR $<0.05$, and every one
was a carbon-starvation or carbon-supply node. The eight C$_2$ photorespiratory intermediates
were not submitted as changed, because the model predicted almost no change in them
(log$_2$FC $0.003$), and none entered the hub analysis, so it says nothing about them.

\subsection{Illumination separates six contrasts; the enclosure gradient does not}

The model's claim is an ordering, so we tested it across six matched flight-versus-ground
contrasts from five studies, spanning three enclosure classes \citep{osd38,osd321,osd678,osd427};
OSD-678 contributes a lit and a dark arm
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}; selection and exclusions in Table~\ref{tab:studies}).

On the starvation-over-photosynthesis contrast, illumination separated them completely:
all three illuminated contrasts were positive ($+0.41$, $+0.92$, $+2.58$) and all three dark
ones negative ($-0.39$, $-1.95$, $-3.22$); one-sided Mann--Whitney $p = 0.05$, the floor
attainable at three versus three. The model predicted no signal in the dark, not an inverse,
and the significant inversions in the two BRIC-PDFU studies are unexplained. In OSD-678's dark
arm the starvation markers still rose ($+2.07$, $p = 0.007$); its negative value comes from
photosystem genes rising further, and is not significant ($p = 0.12$). The separation is not
complete on every contrast: on photorespiration against carbon fixation, OSD-678's dark arm
sits above every lit one. The cleanest evidence is internal: OSD-678 light ($+2.58$) against
OSD-678 dark ($-0.39$) is the same hardware, genotype and flight. Two further caveats bear on
the illumination reading. OSD-38's dark condition is inferred from its hardware rather than
stated, and OSD-678's lit arm grew in diffuse cabin light of 4--6\,\textmu mol
m$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$, an order of magnitude below OSD-522, yet shows the strongest signature.

The predicted gradient across enclosure classes did \emph{not} hold (Spearman
$\rho = -0.50$, $p = 0.67$). Sealed and ventilated fell the right way round but the CARA
plates, modelled as tape-sealed, sat highest, and three studies cannot rank three classes. We report this as a failure of
that prediction. The driver appears to be illumination inside an enclosure rather than sealing
as such --- which is mechanistically what the model says, since drawdown requires
photosynthesis to be running.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The strongest signal in the OSD-522 integration is one this model does not explain: protein
processing in the endoplasmic reticulum, at $p = 8.6\times10^{-9}$, the unfolded protein
response. A gas-transport account explains part of this experiment, not all of it.

What the analysis does support is narrower and, we think, more useful. In an illuminated
enclosure the atmosphere the plants experience is set by the hardware, it is not measured, and
it is large. Because it acts on both arms it does not bias the published contrast --- which is
precisely why it has gone unnoticed. What it does instead is set the physiological state in
which the microgravity response is read out: not a healthy plant, but a carbon-starved one.
That is a baseline condition, not a bias to correct for, and a sensor would settle it.

\subsection{Limitations}

Cross-study comparison is the weakest joint here: different laboratories, durations,
genotypes and harvest protocols. We restricted to GeneLab-standard RNA-seq, aerial tissue
where a study offered it, and matched contrasts, and we report gene-set \emph{ordering} rather
than absolute effect sizes, but the limitation stands. Two of the three dark studies are whole
seedlings, so tissue is partly confounded with illumination. The illumination result rests on
three contrasts per group, from five studies. The predictions and the tests that score them
entered the repository in the same commit, so the record cannot show that the predictions
were fixed first. Pathway enrichment is reported at uncorrected $p$; after FDR correction six
of 232 pathways remain, none of them one the model made a claim about. The enclosure drawdown
and carbon-gain figures rest on an assumed headspace volume and tape permeance, and OSDR does
not describe how the CARA plates were sealed. The intended ventilated proteome arm does not exist, because OSD-16 has no processed
files, so the two-layer comparison is confined to sealed hardware. The metabolite layer is
predicted throughout, and the metabolite class-activity test is close to circular because it
tests our own predicted list; the hub analysis is not, and is reported as the informative one.
The photosynthesis model is solved at saturating irradiance, 1{,}000\,\textmu mol
m$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$, whereas OSD-522 was grown at 60. At 60 the same calculation puts the
oxygenation fraction at 18\,\% in ventilated hardware at ambient CO$_2$ and 46\,\% at
100\,ppm, which at that light is the compensation point, and the microgravity assimilation
deficit at under 3\,\% at leaf and rosette scale and up to 11\,\% in a dense canopy,
growing as CO$_2$ falls rather than holding flat. The direction of each prediction is unchanged, but the magnitudes in
Table~\ref{tab:operating} and Fig.~\ref{fig:fvcb} are saturating-light values
(\texttt{results/tables/T13\_light\_sensitivity.tsv}).
Finally, $V_{cmax}$, $J_{max}$, $R_d$, $g_s$ and $g_m$ remain literature values for
\textit{Arabidopsis} rather than fits to flight material, and $g_s$ is held fixed although
BRIC canisters are humid.

\subsection{What would settle it}

Three things, in increasing cost. First, log CO$_2$ and O$_2$ inside the canister: the central
assumption of this analysis becomes a measurement, and an entire archive of BRIC-derived
transcriptomes becomes reinterpretable. Second, run a targeted metabolite panel on archived
flight material --- roughly twenty compounds would be the first plant spaceflight metabolome
in existence, and our predicted set is a ready-made target list. Third, fly a three-arm design
(Fig.~\ref{fig:implications}): flight sealed, ground sealed, ground ventilated. Two arms
confound hardware with gravity; three separate them, and the third arm never leaves the
ground.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

Full methods, every script and every derived table are in the repository accompanying this
manuscript. Study selection, including each exclusion and its reason, is in
\texttt{data/study\_registry.tsv}.

\textbf{Gas transport.} Boundary-layer conductance was taken from the lattice-Boltzmann
solver in LunarLeaf-CFD \citep{lunarleaf}, from its steady-state export (commit
\texttt{e298785}, regenerated with \texttt{validation/export\_cfd.ts}), and the enclosure drawdown time and 12\,h carbon
gain from its zero-dimensional models (\texttt{validation/hardware\_analysis.py},
\texttt{validation/feedback\_0d.py}), which assume a 30\,mL dish headspace and a tape
permeance; all were vendored into this repository.

\textbf{Photosynthesis model.} A Farquhar--von Caemmerer--Berry C$_3$ model \citep{farquhar1980}
with the oxygenation term explicit, using in-vivo temperature responses
\citep{bernacchi2001,bernacchi2003}, solved for the supply-equals-demand operating point.
Every operating point, assimilation deficit and metabolite prediction reported here is solved
at an irradiance of 1{,}000\,\textmu mol m$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$ (\texttt{scripts/fvcb.py}).

\textbf{Differential expression.} OSD-522 was processed with PyDESeq2 \citep{pydeseq2}. The
other five studies use GeneLab's own deposited differential-expression tables, with matched
flight-versus-ground contrasts selected by pattern and recorded in the registry.

\textbf{Gene sets.} Built from KEGG pathway membership and description regular expressions
fetched live \citep{kanehisa2000,kegg}, never typed from memory.

\textbf{Pathway integration.} PaintOmics \citep{paintomics4}, organism \texttt{ath}, KEGG and
MapMan, with the optional AI interpretation disabled. The jobs were first run on a metabolite
layer predicted from an earlier LunarLeaf-CFD export (photosynthate log$_2$FC $-0.114$ against
$-0.131$ now) and resubmitted on the current one; every pathway, per-omic and hub $p$-value was
identical. PaintOmics reports enrichment but not
the gene lists behind it, and MapMan has no equivalent of the KEGG REST API, so the gene
membership used in the accompanying data explorer was reconstructed from GoMapMan's
Araport11 gene-to-bin mapping and the MapMan diagram layouts, by PaintOmics' own inclusion
rules. PaintOmics matches a diagram's bin codes anywhere in a gene's bin code, not only from
the start, so its MapMan diagrams hold more than their names suggest: \textit{photosynthesis}
has 1{,}722 measured loci this way and 488 if matched from the start. It reproduces PaintOmics'
feature count exactly or within three features for 9 of the 13 significant MapMan diagrams
and runs larger for the four largest;
both counts are reported rather than one, and the discrepancy on those four is not
explained by any rule recoverable from PaintOmics' published sources.
