\title[Gas transport predicts the spaceflight transcriptome]{A gas-transport model predicts the \textit{Arabidopsis} spaceflight

\abstract{Plants flown on the International Space Station are grown inside enclosures, and
the atmosphere inside those enclosures is rarely measured. We coupled a validated
lattice-Boltzmann model of leaf boundary-layer gas transport to a Farquhar--von
Caemmerer--Berry model of C$_3$ photosynthesis and asked what an illuminated, sealed canister
does to the CO$_2$ reaching Rubisco. The model predicts that photosynthesis draws a sealed
Biological Research in Canisters (BRIC) enclosure down to near its CO$_2$ compensation point
within minutes, raising the Rubisco oxygenation fraction from 28\,\% to 54\,\% and reducing
12\,h carbon gain to 1\,\% of an unenclosed control. Because that drawdown is a mass balance,
it occurs in the ground control as well as in flight. It therefore does not bias the
flight-versus-ground contrast every such experiment reports --- it changes what that contrast
is \emph{about}. We estimate the enclosure effect on metabolite pools to be 25 times the
microgravity effect, which means the spaceflight response is being measured in plants their
own hardware has already carbon-starved.
Contrary to our own starting hypothesis, the microgravity boundary-layer penalty does not
amplify as CO$_2$ falls; it converges, leaving a persistent 5--9\,\% assimilation deficit.
Fixing these predictions before examining any data, we tested them against OSD-522
(BRIC-LED), where the model called eight of nine gene-set responses correctly, and against
five further OSDR studies. Illumination separated the six studies perfectly --- every
illuminated enclosure showed a carbon-starvation signature and every dark one its inverse ---
while the predicted gradient across enclosure types did not hold. NASA's Open Science Data
Repository contains no plant metabolome at all (0 of 66 plant studies), so the third omic
layer for pathway integration had to be predicted rather than measured. We argue that
enclosure atmosphere is a first-order, currently unmeasured covariate in plant spaceflight
biology --- not a bias to correct for, but a baseline state to measure --- and set out the
three-arm design and the in-canister measurement that would establish it.}

\keywords{spaceflight, microgravity, photorespiration, \textit{Arabidopsis},
growth hardware, multi-omics}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Interpreting a plant spaceflight experiment means attributing a molecular difference to
microgravity. That attribution rests on an assumption that is almost never tested: that the
flight and ground arms differ in gravity and in nothing else that matters to metabolism.

Plants flown on the International Space Station are grown inside enclosures --- sealed
Petri Dish Fixation Units in BRIC canisters, micropore-taped dishes in CARA, actively
ventilated chambers in VEGGIE and the Advanced Plant Habitat. These enclosures differ by
orders of magnitude in how freely they exchange gas with the cabin, and to our knowledge no
BRIC-class flight has logged the CO$_2$ concentration its plants actually experienced.

The quantity that matters is not the cabin atmosphere but the CO$_2$ mole fraction reaching
Rubisco, $C_c$, which sits at the end of a chain of conductances: from bulk air across the
leaf boundary layer, through the stomata, and through the mesophyll. Only the first of those
is gravity-dependent, through buoyancy-driven convection. Rubisco partitions between
carboxylation and oxygenation as $V_o/V_c = 2\Gamma^*/C_c$ \citep{farquhar1980}, so anything
that lowers $C_c$ raises photorespiration.

Here we ask a question about the enclosure rather than about gravity, and we ask it of the
model before we ask it of any data.

\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{An illuminated sealed canister is a CO\texorpdfstring{$_2$}{2}-starvation chamber}

Gas transport was taken from LunarLeaf-CFD \citep{lunarleaf}, a lattice-Boltzmann solver
validated against four analytical and empirical gates. Its enclosure mass balance draws a lit
BRIC canister from 400\,ppm to near zero in approximately seven minutes, and puts 12\,h carbon
gain at 1\,\% of an unenclosed control against 90\,\% for micropore tape and 100\,\% when
ventilated (Fig.~\ref{fig:hardware}).

The step that matters for interpretation is that this drawdown is a mass balance between
photosynthetic uptake and enclosure volume. It is therefore essentially gravity-independent,
and it happens in the ground control as much as in flight.

\subsection{What that does to Rubisco, and what gravity does not do}

Feeding the boundary-layer conductance into a Farquhar--von Caemmerer--Berry model
\citep{farquhar1980} with in-vivo Rubisco kinetics \citep{bernacchi2001,bernacchi2003} moves
the leaf along its CO$_2$ response curve: oxygenation fraction rises from
28.3\,\% in ventilated hardware at ambient CO$_2$ to 53.7\,\% in a drawn-down canister
(Fig.~\ref{fig:fvcb}a, Table~\ref{tab:operating}).

We began from the hypothesis that CO$_2$ starvation would \emph{amplify} the microgravity
boundary-layer penalty. The model says the opposite (Fig.~\ref{fig:fvcb}b). As assimilation
falls toward the compensation point, the flux through the boundary layer falls with it, so
the CO$_2$ drop across that layer, $A/g_{bl}$, tends to zero and the two gravities converge.
What survives is a persistent proportional assimilation deficit of about 5\,\% at leaf scale,
7\,\% at rosette scale and 9\,\% in a dense canopy, nearly independent of where in the
drawdown the plant sits (Fig.~\ref{fig:fvcb}c). That insensitivity is the prediction's
strength: it does not require knowing the canister CO$_2$, which nobody measured.

Propagating both contrasts to metabolite pools makes the asymmetry explicit. The
sealed-versus-ventilated hardware effect is 25 times the flight-versus-ground effect
(Fig.~\ref{fig:fvcb}d) --- and because it acts on both arms, it cancels.

\subsection{A metabolite layer that does not exist}

Pathway-level integration needs a metabolome. We queried all 567 OSDR studies and
cross-tabulated organism against assay type: six are metabolite profiling, and every one is
mouse, human, rat or microbial. \textbf{None of the 66 plant studies has a metabolome.}

We therefore predicted one (Fig.~\ref{fig:metabolome}), propagating the modelled fluxes to 21
compounds under one stated assumption --- that at quasi-steady state a pool tracks the flux
feeding it. Every value is labelled as model output in every file. Compounds the model cannot
speak to, including all tricarboxylic-acid intermediates and all fermentation products, are
excluded with the reason recorded rather than filled in.

\subsection{The model called eight of nine gene sets, blind}

OSD-522 \citep{osd522} grew \textit{Arabidopsis} in BRIC-LED hardware under 60\,\textmu mol
m$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$ of red and blue light, with six flight and six ground-control shoot
samples. We computed differential expression with PyDESeq2 \citep{pydeseq2}, obtaining 1{,}795
genes at FDR $<0.05$ from 20{,}983 tested, and used the deposited proteomics ratios for 5{,}160
proteins (Fig.~\ref{fig:omics}).

Scoring these against KEGG-derived gene sets (Fig.~\ref{fig:blind}), the model's predictions
held for eight of nine sets: the photosystem and light-harvesting apparatus fell
($-0.238$, $p = 3.8\times10^{-6}$; and in the proteome $-0.820$, $p = 5.0\times10^{-4}$),
Rubisco fell ($-0.403$, $p = 4.5\times10^{-3}$), carbon fixation and starch/sucrose
metabolism fell, carbon-starvation DIN markers rose ($+0.686$, $p = 2.6\times10^{-3}$), and
photorespiratory enzymes were not induced. The one mismatch is the whole KEGG glyoxylate and
dicarboxylate map, which contains a good deal besides the C$_2$ cycle.

The sharper test is between sets, which is immune to the global depression of metabolic
transcripts: photorespiratory enzymes sat above Rubisco in the transcriptome
($+0.239$, $p = 6.8\times10^{-3}$) and independently in the proteome
($+0.285$, $p = 4.9\times10^{-2}$).

\subsection{Pathway integration ranks photorespiration last of 231}

Submitting all three layers to PaintOmics \citep{paintomics4} against KEGG and MapMan
(Fig.~\ref{fig:paintomics}), 98--100\,\% of genes, 97\,\% of proteins and all 21 predicted
compounds mapped. Of 231 pathways tested, 31 reached combined Fisher $p<0.05$, including
photosynthesis ($p = 0.008$), photosynthesis antenna proteins ($p = 0.022$) and starch and
sucrose metabolism ($p = 0.019$). Glyoxylate and dicarboxylate metabolism --- the
photorespiration map --- ranked \emph{last of all 231} at $p = 0.994$.

The metabolite hub analysis is the least circular result available here, because the
compounds are predicted but the differentially expressed genes counted around them are
measured. Seven of fifteen compounds reached FDR $<0.05$, and every one was a
carbon-starvation or carbon-supply node. Not one of the eight C$_2$ photorespiratory
intermediates was a significant hub, although all eight mapped.

\subsection{Illumination separates six studies; the enclosure gradient does not}

The model's claim is an ordering, so we tested it across six matched flight-versus-ground
contrasts spanning three enclosure classes \citep{osd38,osd321,osd678,osd427}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}; selection and exclusions in Table~\ref{tab:studies}).

Illumination separated them completely. All three illuminated studies showed the
starvation-over-photosynthesis signature ($+0.41$, $+0.92$, $+2.58$) and all three dark
studies its inverse ($-0.39$, $-1.95$, $-3.22$); one-sided Mann--Whitney $p = 0.05$, the floor
attainable at three versus three. The cleanest evidence is internal: OSD-678 light ($+2.58$)
against OSD-678 dark ($-0.39$) is the same hardware, genotype and flight.

The predicted gradient across enclosure classes did \emph{not} hold (Spearman
$\rho = -0.50$, $p = 0.67$). Sealed and ventilated fell the right way round but micropore
tape sat highest, and three studies cannot rank three classes. We report this as a failure of
that prediction. The driver appears to be illumination inside an enclosure rather than sealing
as such --- which is mechanistically what the model says, since drawdown requires
photosynthesis to be running.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The strongest signal in the OSD-522 integration is one this model does not explain: protein
processing in the endoplasmic reticulum, at $p = 8.6\times10^{-9}$, the unfolded protein
response. A gas-transport account explains part of this experiment, not all of it.

What the analysis does support is narrower and, we think, more useful. In an illuminated
enclosure the atmosphere the plants experience is set by the hardware, it is not measured, and
it is large. Because it acts on both arms it does not bias the published contrast --- which is
precisely why it has gone unnoticed. What it does instead is set the physiological state in
which the microgravity response is read out: not a healthy plant, but a carbon-starved one.
That is a baseline condition, not a bias to correct for, and a sensor would settle it.

\subsection{Limitations}

Cross-study comparison is the weakest joint here: different laboratories, durations,
genotypes and harvest protocols. We restricted to GeneLab-standard RNA-seq, above-ground
tissue and matched contrasts, and we report gene-set \emph{ordering} rather than absolute
effect sizes, but the limitation stands. The illumination result rests on three studies per
group. The intended ventilated proteome arm does not exist, because OSD-16 has no processed
files, so the two-layer comparison is confined to sealed hardware. The metabolite layer is
predicted throughout, and the metabolite class-activity test is close to circular because it
tests our own predicted list; the hub analysis is not, and is reported as the informative one.
Finally, $V_{cmax}$, $J_{max}$, $R_d$, $g_s$ and $g_m$ remain literature values for
\textit{Arabidopsis} rather than fits to flight material, and $g_s$ is held fixed although
BRIC canisters are humid.

\subsection{What would settle it}

Three things, in increasing cost. First, log CO$_2$ and O$_2$ inside the canister: the central
assumption of this analysis becomes a measurement, and an entire archive of BRIC-derived
transcriptomes becomes reinterpretable. Second, run a targeted metabolite panel on archived
flight material --- roughly twenty compounds would be the first plant spaceflight metabolome
in existence, and our predicted set is a ready-made target list. Third, fly a three-arm design
(Fig.~\ref{fig:implications}): flight sealed, ground sealed, ground ventilated. Two arms
confound hardware with gravity; three separate them, and the third arm never leaves the
ground.

\section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

Full methods, every script and every derived table are in the repository accompanying this
manuscript. Study selection, including each exclusion and its reason, is in
\texttt{data/study\_registry.tsv}.

\textbf{Gas transport.} Boundary-layer conductance and enclosure mass balances were taken from
LunarLeaf-CFD \citep{lunarleaf} and vendored into this repository.

\textbf{Photosynthesis model.} A Farquhar--von Caemmerer--Berry C$_3$ model \citep{farquhar1980}
with the oxygenation term explicit, using in-vivo temperature responses
\citep{bernacchi2001,bernacchi2003}, solved for the supply-equals-demand operating point.

\textbf{Differential expression.} OSD-522 was processed with PyDESeq2 \citep{pydeseq2}. The
other five studies use GeneLab's own deposited differential-expression tables, with matched
flight-versus-ground contrasts selected by pattern and recorded in the registry.

\textbf{Gene sets.} Built from KEGG pathway membership and description regular expressions
fetched live \citep{kegg}, never typed from memory.

\textbf{Pathway integration.} PaintOmics \citep{paintomics4}, organism \texttt{ath}, KEGG and
MapMan, with the optional AI interpretation disabled. PaintOmics reports enrichment but not
the gene lists behind it, and MapMan has no equivalent of the KEGG REST API, so the gene
membership used in the accompanying data explorer was reconstructed from GoMapMan's
Araport11 gene-to-bin mapping and the MapMan diagram layouts, by PaintOmics' own inclusion
rules. It reproduces PaintOmics' feature count exactly or within three features for 9 of the
13 significant MapMan diagrams and runs larger for the four whole-ontology overview maps;
both counts are reported rather than one, and the discrepancy on those four is not
explained by any rule recoverable from PaintOmics' published sources.
