\begin{figure}[htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figS1_sankey.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{Where the loci go, and how far the MapMan half of the diagram can be
trusted.}
\textbf{a}, Each locus in at least one of the 28 non-whole-ontology significant pathways is
placed in one transcript bin and one protein bin, and then into the pathways it belongs to.
Ribbon width is a locus count. The transcript and protein columns conserve loci exactly; the
pathway column counts \emph{memberships}, so a locus in more than one pathway is counted more
than once. The pathway column draws the ten most significant; a square gives each pathway's
database in the colours of Fig.~\ref{fig:paintomics}a. Three whole-ontology MapMan diagrams
are excluded --- \textit{Overview} lists all 36 top-level bins and so covers 99.8\,\% of
measured loci, and \textit{Regulation overview} and \textit{Biotic Stress} cover 45\,\% and
44\,\%; the next largest covers 17\,\%. A map holding nearly every measured locus cannot say
where anything goes, because its ribbons reproduce the marginal distribution.
\textbf{b}, PaintOmics reports enrichment but not the gene lists behind it, and MapMan has no
equivalent of the KEGG REST API, so MapMan membership is reconstructed from GoMapMan's
Araport11 gene-to-bin mapping and the MapMan diagram layouts, by PaintOmics' own inclusion
rules. Reconstructed membership is compared against the feature count PaintOmics itself
reported for each diagram (log scale). Nine of the thirteen agree to within three features.
The four that do not are the largest maps, are marked in vermilion with the ratio between the
two counts, and the reason for the difference is not recoverable from PaintOmics' published
sources; both counts are reported rather than one.}
\label{fig:sankey}
\end{figure}
